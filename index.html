Deponent Verb Synopsis

KeyPractice
IChoose your verb
Remember: a deponent is forma passiva, significātiō activa. Every translation is active (ingrediuntur = "they enter"), except the gerundive (FPP), which keeps a passive meaning.
Deponent rules (quick reference)
  • Present system (present, imperfect, future, present and imperfect subjunctive): passive endings on the present stem.
  • Perfect system: perfect participle + a form of sum. The participle agrees with the subject: singular -us/-a/-um, plural -ī/-ae/-a.
  • Future in the 3rd, 3rd -iō and 4th: -ar, -ēris, -ētur (sequar, ingrediar, potiar), never -bor.
  • Imperfect subjunctive: looks like an active infinitive + passive endings (sequerer, ingrederer, mīrārer).
  • Participles on this sheet: the PPP (passive in form, active in meaning: "having entered") and the gerundive / FPP (-ndus, the only form with a passive meaning: "to be entered").
  • Infinitives: present -ārī/-ērī/-ī/-īrī; perfect = PPP + esse; future = -ūrus + esse (not -um īrī).
  • Imperatives: singular ends in -re (sequere, mīrāre), plural in -minī (sequiminī).
  • Irregular future forms: morior → moritūrus; nāscor → nāscitūrus; fruor → fruitūrus.
Grading: macrons, capitals, extra spaces and punctuation don't matter. Translations accept common English variants. If a translation you know is right gets marked wrong, use "count it". Your mistakes and answers are saved in this browser.

Dropped sounds and letters

Two different processes

Poetic contraction shortens a written word: vincula → vincla, repositum → repostum, audīverat → audierat.

Elision happens in recitation: a final vowel, or vowel + m, is not fully sounded before a following vowel or h.
